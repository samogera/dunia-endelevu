Is climate change fair?

Climate justice is about fairness: who caused the problem, who is hurt most, and who gets a say.

Not everyone caused it equally

Countries that burned the most coal, oil and gas for many years caused most of climate change. Africa produces only about 4 out of every 100 parts of the world’s greenhouse gases.

Not everyone is hurt equally either

Many communities in Africa face droughts and floods, even though they did little to cause them. Families who farm, keep animals or live near rivers often feel it first.

Children have the most at stake

Children did the least to cause climate change, but they will live with it the longest. Yet they are rarely asked what they think when big decisions are made.

What climate justice asks for

Those who caused more do more to cut pollution and to help others get ready. People most at risk are protected first. And everyone, including children, gets a say.

“Children aren’t just victims of climate change. We’re problem solvers.”

Alana Chelangat, founder of Dunia Endelevu

Words to know

Justice (haki)
Fairness. In Kiswahili, haki also means rights.
Emissions
Gases released into the air, like smoke from burning fuel.
Climate finance
Money that helps countries cut pollution and get ready for climate change.
Vulnerable
More likely to be harmed.
