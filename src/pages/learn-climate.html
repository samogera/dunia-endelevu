Why is our planet getting warmer?

Climate change explained with a blanket, a sunny day and a few simple ideas.

Weather or climate?

Weather is what the sky is doing today: sunny, cloudy, rainy or windy. Climate is the usual weather of a place over many years, usually 30 years or more.

One hot day is weather. Hotter years, again and again, are a change in climate.

Earth’s warm blanket

Earth is wrapped in a thin layer of air. Some gases in that air, such as carbon dioxide, work like a blanket: they keep some of the sun’s heat in.

That is a good thing. Without it, Earth would be far too cold for people, plants and animals.

Why the blanket is getting thicker

When people burn coal, oil and gas for cars, factories and electricity, they add more carbon dioxide to the air. Cutting down forests adds more too, because trees soak it up.

A thicker blanket traps more heat, so Earth warms. It is now more than 1°C warmer than about 150 years ago. That sounds small, but for a whole planet it is a lot.

What it means in Kenya

A warmer planet changes the rains. Some places get longer dry spells, which are hard for farmers and herders. Others get heavier rain and floods. Very hot days come more often too.

The good news

We know what causes climate change, so we know how to slow it. Kenya already makes most of its electricity from clean sources, like geothermal steam from under the Rift Valley, water and wind.

Planting trees, saving energy and walking or cycling all help.

Words to know

Weather
What the sky is doing today.
Climate
The usual weather of a place over many years.
Carbon dioxide
A gas in the air. Burning coal, oil and gas makes lots of it.
Greenhouse effect
The way some gases keep heat in, like a blanket.
Fossil fuels
Coal, oil and gas, formed from plants and animals that lived long ago.
